The Story of Amani Jordan Dev

A self-taught front-end developer born in Bukavu, raised in Uganda, and still building.

8 min read  |  By Amani Jordan Dev

My name is Amani Jordan, known online as Amani Jordan Dev. I was born on 8 July 2007 in Bukavu, Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC). Although I was born in Congo, my journey took me to Uganda while I was still young. Uganda became the place where I grew up, learned, studied, discovered technology, and began building the person I am today.

From Bukavu to Uganda

My story started in Bukavu, but much of my childhood was shaped by Uganda. I came here young, and as I grew up, Uganda became home. I studied here for about five years and completed Senior Four (S4), earning my S4 certificate.

But school was only one part of my education. Another education was happening through my curiosity. From a young age, I was fascinated by technology. I loved discovering how things worked and imagining what could be created with a computer.

I didn't just want to use technology. I wanted to create with it.

The beginning of my love for coding

I was drawn to coding because it gave me something powerful: the ability to turn an idea in my head into something people could actually see and use. An idea could begin as nothing more than a thought. Then I could write some code, the screen would change, something would start working, and an idea that existed only in my imagination had become real.

That feeling is one of the reasons I fell in love with development. Coding isn't just about writing lines of code. For me, it is about bringing ideas to life.

Becoming self-taught

My journey into technology has largely been self-driven. I didn't wait until I had everything figured out before starting. I learned by experimenting, making mistakes, searching for answers, building projects, breaking things, fixing them, and trying again.

I started with HTML, CSS and JavaScript, gradually learning how websites work and how to make them interactive, responsive and useful. Sometimes a project worked. Sometimes it didn't, and I would spend hours trying to understand why. But every problem became part of my education.

Being a developer isn't about knowing everything. It's about being willing to learn what you don't know.

From learning code to building ideas

As I became more comfortable with development, I stopped wanting to build things only for practice. I wanted to build things that had meaning, around real ideas and real problems. Each project represents a different part of my journey: some helped me understand JavaScript, some responsive design, some challenged me to think about users, and others made me think about businesses and communities.

Amani Message Hub

The idea came from something simple: sometimes people know what they feel but don't know exactly how to express it. A person may need a birthday message, a word of encouragement for a friend, a prayer, a motivational message, or simply the right words for a particular moment. I wanted to create something that could help people find those words. That became Amani Message Hub.

Projects like this show why I love technology. An idea can start with one question: "What if I created something that could help people with this?" And then the development journey begins.

FixWork

FixWork represents a bigger vision. It is built around connecting people who need services with skilled workers who can provide them. The concept comes from the environment I grew up around in Uganda, where many talented people know how to repair, build, install, design, clean and maintain things, while others are looking for someone to do those jobs. Technology can help connect those two sides.

FixWork gave me the chance to think beyond the interface and start thinking about real-world problems, users and opportunities.

Discovering the power of community

As I continued developing, I realized that technology becomes even more powerful when people learn and build together. I became interested not only in becoming a better developer myself, but also in creating spaces where other developers could learn, connect, share ideas and grow. That thinking led to DevRise Community and my work around the Code Investors identity. Development could become a community journey, not only an individual one.

Building in public

Another important part of my story is documenting what I learn. I joined the developer community on DEV and began sharing my journey publicly. Writing about what I was learning forced me to think more deeply about it. When you explain something to another person, you discover how much you understand and how much you still need to learn.

So I started sharing. Not because I had mastered everything, but because I believe people can grow together while they are still on the journey.

JEZBuilders

One of the newer chapters of my journey is JEZBuilders, which brings developers and designers together with a shared vision of building digital products. At the beginning, my main question was "How can I learn to code?" Then it became "What can I build?" Now another question is becoming important: "What can we build together?"

Working with other developers and designers has taught me that software development isn't only about technical skills. It is about communication, Git and GitHub, dividing responsibilities, reviewing each other's work, listening to different ideas, and understanding the problem before trying to build the solution. Where I am today

Today, I see myself as a young developer who is still learning, still experimenting and still building. I am especially interested in front-end development, JavaScript, responsive design and modern web applications, while continuing to grow with React and the wider modern JavaScript ecosystem.

But I don't want my identity to be limited to the title "front-end developer." I want to be a builder: someone who can see a problem, imagine a solution, learn whatever is necessary, and turn that solution into something real.

My journey is still beginning

I was born in Bukavu, DRC, on 8 July 2007. I grew up in Uganda. I studied in Uganda. I completed my S4 certificate. And somewhere along that journey, I discovered technology. What started as curiosity became coding. Coding became projects. Projects became ideas. Ideas became products. And products began becoming a vision.

I don't consider myself finished. I believe I'm still at the beginning. There are technologies I haven't mastered, products I haven't built, problems I haven't discovered, people I haven't met, and ideas that haven't yet left my imagination. That's what makes the journey exciting.

My vision

My dream is not simply to know how to code. I want to create things that matter. I want to build technology that helps people, create products from ideas others might think are impossible, work with talented people, and build communities where young developers can learn without feeling alone.

And I want other young people from Africa to see that they can learn technology, build products and create opportunities from wherever they are.

An idea doesn't have to remain an idea. If you are willing to learn, experiment, fail, try again and keep building, you can bring it to life.

I am Amani Jordan Dev. And I'm still building.
